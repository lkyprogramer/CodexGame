Yes.<channel|>When benchmarking DiffusionGemma Q4_K_M on an RTX 4090, the performance tradeoff between autoregressive decoding and diffusion generation centers on memory bandwidth versus computational throughput. Autoregressive decoding is primarily memory-bandwidth bound; because each token is generated sequentially based on the previous ones, the GPU must reload the quantized weights from VRAM for every single token produced. On a 4090, while the bandwidth is high, the sequential nature limits the "tokens per second" metric.

In contrast, diffusion generation is typically compute-bound. While it requires multiple iterative denoising steps to refine the output, each step involves heavy tensor operations that can be highly parallelized across the GPU's thousands of CUDA cores. Consequently, the latency in diffusion is driven by the number of denoising steps required rather than the length of the sequence being generated. DG_OUTPUT_N1024. Ultimately, the tradeoff reveals that autoregressive models suffer from high latency in long sequences, whereas diffusion models offer better flexibility in high-dimensional generation but are constrained by the total iteration count needed for convergence.
